## How bus times are worked out

Bus times are worked out the way [bustimes.org](https://bustimes.org) does it, from the Bus Open Data Service (BODS): the timetable for your stops, plus where the buses are right now. BODS covers England only; elsewhere, `source = "transportapi"` under `[bus]` takes bus times from TransportAPI alone. The setup wizard sets this up for you; in `config.toml` it looks like this:

```toml
[bus]
source = "bods"
operators = ["BNML", "HIPK"]    # the bus companies serving your stops (these are Glossop's)
interval_minutes = 2
windows = ["Mon-Sun 06:00-23:00"]
```

- **The timetable** is built on the Pi by `twobells-timetable.timer`. It checks every hour and rebuilds weekly, when a timetable in it ends, or when your stops or operators change. A build that fails is tried again 6 hours later, or at once if your stops, operators or BODS key change (a setup search cancelled while it builds hasn't failed, so doesn't wait); until then the board keeps the timetable it has. It downloads the operators' timetables from BODS and keeps only the journeys calling at your stops, with the days each runs: school-term-only journeys run only in the terms the timetable gives, and bank holidays follow the operator's own rules. A timetable file too big to read in the memory there is (on a Pi, over 61 MB; in Docker, over 100 MB; on a desktop, over 200 MB) is skipped with a warning in the log naming it, and the rest are used. The largest seen is 48 MB (First Bus's, in October 2026). For the same reason a build holds at most 8,500 journeys calling at your stops on a Pi, 8,000 in Docker and 20,000 on a desktop, counted as it reads them, old versions of a timetable included, and fails with a message in the log if there are more (a busy stop's week has a few thousand at most); of the journeys ending near your stop (below), it keeps at most half that number and leaves out the rest. Where BODS still has an old version of a timetable beside its replacement (the same service with a higher revision number, as bustimes.org reads them), the old one ends the day before the new one starts, so no bus shows twice; a temporary timetable (for roadworks, say) takes over only for its own dates, and the usual one runs again after it. Versions of the same revision are parts of one timetable, and all of them run: High Peak's 393 has its Saturdays in one, from 2024, and its weekdays in another, from 2026. It also keeps journeys of the same lines that end at a stand within 150 m of your stop without calling at it, only to spot the bus coming in to work a departure from your stop; they never show as departures. A release that keeps the timetable differently (0.2.0 does, for the buses coming in to turn round, and 0.2.2, for timetables with more than one version) builds it again once after updating: until then the settings' Overview says **Out of date**: *Due to be built again: it was built by an older version*, and the timetable it has is still used. On a Pi the dashboard's feeds read this timetable and never build it; while a bus company search runs there, the dashboard shows its buses as being built and lets go of the timetable, so the search's build has the memory, and shows them again once the search is done.
- **Each check**, the board asks BODS where those lines' buses are, matches each bus to its timetabled journey, places it on the road between that journey's stops, and shows how late it is there. A bus is placed when it is heading the right way within 300 m of the road the timetable saved for the bus maps, or within 150 m of a straight line between two stops it has no road for; a bus whose tracker last reported over 10 minutes ago isn't used. The timetable keeps where the stops are for the 20 stops before each of yours and the one after. A line that turns round at your stop (its journeys end there, or within 150 m, and it has departures starting there) has every stop of those journeys kept, so the bus coming in to work a departure is seen all the way in, and BODS is asked for the buses in a box around all of them. Those further out than the 20 stops before yours only follow the bus in: [disruptions](dashboard.md#disruptions-and-engineering-works) there aren't counted as near your stops. A box bigger than 500 km² (Glossop's is 16 by 28 km, 450 km²) is drawn round the 20 stops before yours only: BODS sends every bus in the box, and over a city that would be more than the board takes, losing every check's live positions. A bus running early is shown on time, since buses wait at timing points. A bus without a live position shows its timetable time with `sch`.
- **A bus coming in to turn round.** A departure that starts at your stop can't be tracked until its bus turns round there: until then it is working the journey before. Where the operator's timetable gives blocks (the journeys one bus works in a day, TransXChange's `BlockNumber`), the board follows that bus in: if it is placed on the journey before this one in its block, the departure counts as live, marked inbound ("Bus on its way, due in 6 min"), and a late inbound bus makes it late, leaving 2 minutes after it gets in, never before its time. A bus already in goes any minute once the departure's time has gone, for up to 30 minutes; still in after that, it has laid over or gone out of service, and the departure goes. The same bus goes with the journey to its later stops of yours until it is tracked on the journey itself: each is live too, expected the timetable's time from the first stop after the bus is expected to leave there ("Bus on its way, due in 9 min", then "Bus waiting to start" while it is in at the first stop), and each goes when the first stop's does. The punctuality history records no delay for a bus only waiting in. A bus sending that block as its BlockRef counts too, when no journey matches it. The block is checked, not trusted: the journey before must be the same operator's, on the same day, and end at your stop (or within 150 m of it) up to 30 minutes before. If that bus isn't seen, the departure stays timetable only. Many operators give no blocks (Bee Network's and High Peak's timetables had none in October 2026); then the board guesses at the bus coming in by line, as bustimes.org does: each bus placed on a journey of the line and operator ending at your stop (or within 150 m) goes to the earliest departure from there it can make, leaving up to 30 minutes after it is due in, and a departure with exactly one bus coming to it counts as live, marked inbound, just as above. Two buses for one departure, or two departures leaving at the same minute, is too much of a guess, and none is shown. A bus whose block goes on to another journey is left to that one, and a departure first in its block, whose bus comes out of the depot, is never guessed at.
- **The last bus** of each line and destination today comes from the same timetable, with its days of the week and bank holidays, and costs no calls. A day runs past midnight: a bus at 00:20 on a journey that started the evening before is that evening's last bus, and so is one before 03:00 that the timetable gives the next day, as many do; it stays the last bus until it has gone. The dashboard shows it (see [The dashboard](dashboard.md#whats-on-it)), and so does the HDMI screen in the last hour while **Last bus** is ticked under **What to show** (see [Reading the board](reading-the-board.md#the-hdmi-screen)). Only the directions the board shows count, and buses from TransportAPI have no last bus. Trains have no last train: Darwin gives only the next two hours.
- **Building it now**, rather than waiting up to an hour: the service builds only when a build is due, and not within 6 hours of one that failed, so delete the old timetable, and the failed build's note, first. Use the service, which reads the keys in `/etc/twobells/secrets.env` and writes the timetable as the app's user:

  ```bash
  sudo rm -f /var/lib/twobells/bus-timetable.json /var/lib/twobells/bus-timetable-failed.json  # only to rebuild
  sudo systemctl start twobells-timetable.service      # a few minutes on a Zero W
  journalctl -u twobells-timetable -n 5
  ```

  Run by hand with your own config and keys (on a laptop, say), `twobells timetable --force` does both in one step.

## TransportAPI as a stand-in

Until there's a usable timetable (none yet, built for other stops, or not rebuilt for four weeks), TransportAPI stands in on the board if its keys are set, checked every `fallback_interval_minutes` (default 12), within the daily budget, and only inside `fallback_windows`. By default those are the commute, `["Mon-Fri 07:00-08:30", "Mon-Fri 17:00-18:00"]`, which fits 28 lookups with two stops, whatever the bus windows say. Both are set in `config.toml` only. Without TransportAPI keys the board says it isn't updating buses.

## The bus budget

This applies only to TransportAPI: with `source = "bods"`, only while it stands in for the timetable. Its free plan allows 30 lookups a day, and each check costs one per stop. With two stops, checking every 12 minutes from 07:00 to 08:30 and 17:00 to 18:00 uses 28 a day. The board keeps 2 spare for test lookups. It never goes over **Most TransportAPI lookups a day**: once the budget is spent, bus times stop updating until midnight, and after the "not updating" time passes the board says so.

Bus positions for the [screen's bus page](displays.md#the-bus-position-page) come from BODS, with no daily allowance to manage. They're checked every 30 seconds, only during the bus windows, and never written to the SD card.
